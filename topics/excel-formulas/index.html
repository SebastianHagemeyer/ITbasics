1. Your first formula

A formula is an instruction that makes Excel calculate. It always starts with =. Without the =, Excel thinks you've typed some words and just shows them.

SignMeansExample
+add=B4+C4
-subtract=C4-B4
*multiply=B4*2
/divide=B4/3

PixelPanda scored 420, 380 and 510. Click E4 and type a formula that adds up their three rounds using the cell references, then press Enter.

2. Why cell references, not the numbers?

You could type =420+380+510 and get the same answer, 1310. So why bother with =B4+C4+D4? Press the button and find out.

Key idea: a formula with cell references uses whatever is in those cells right now. Change a score and every formula that uses it updates by itself.

3. SUM, MAX, MIN and AVERAGE

A function is a ready-made formula with a name. You give it a range: a block of cells written first:last, so B4:B11 means every cell from B4 down to B11.

Type thisIt works out
=SUM(B4:D4)Adds up every cell from B4 to D4
=MAX(B4:B11)The largest number from B4 to B11
=MIN(B4:B11)The smallest number
=AVERAGE(B4:B11)The average (mean). It's AVERAGE, not AVG.

Fill in B13, B14 and B15 (the outlined cells) for the Round 1 column.

    4. The fill handle

    Typing the same kind of formula eight times is slow. Click a formula cell and you'll see a little square in its bottom-right corner: the fill handle. Drag it down and Excel copies the formula, changing the cell references for each row: E5 gets =SUM(B5:D5), E6 gets =SUM(B6:D6), and so on.

    E4 already has =SUM(B4:D4). Click E4, then drag its fill handle down to E11. (Keyboard way: select E4 to E11, then press Ctrl + D.)

        Glossary

        Formula
        An instruction that makes Excel calculate. Always starts with =.
        Function
        A ready-made formula with a name, like SUM or MAX.
        Range
        A block of cells written first:last. B4:D4 is B4, C4 and D4.
        SUM
        Adds up a range.
        MAX / MIN
        The largest / smallest number in a range.
        AVERAGE
        The mean of a range. Not AVG.
        Fill handle
        The little square at a cell's bottom-right corner. Drag it to copy a formula; the references change for each row or column.
