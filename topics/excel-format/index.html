Make it readable

A grid of numbers is hard work to read. Today you turn your leaderboard into something people actually want to look at, using four tools on Excel's Home tab: Merge & Center, Wrap Text, Borders and Fill Colour.

1. Change the look, not the data

To format a cell is to change how it looks without changing the data inside it. Same scores, same names; just easier to read. Compare these two. Every difference has a job to do for the reader.

Before

After

Check your understanding: You make a cell with the number 510 bold and shade it blue. What is in the cell now?

Formatting changes how the cell looks, never what's in it. It's still the number 510, and formulas can still use it.

2. Merge & Center (and split)

Merge & Center joins several cells into one big cell and centres what's in it. It's how you make a title sit across the top of the whole table. Click it a second time and the cells split back apart.

To select several cells, click the first one and drag to the last (or click the first, then Shift + click the last).

The rule: only merge titles. Never merge cells in the middle of your data: formulas, sorting and filling all trip over merged cells.

    3. Wrap Text

    Long headings are a pain: either they get cut off, or the column has to be huge. Wrap Text lets the words run onto extra lines inside the one cell, so the column can stay narrow. Click the cell, then Wrap Text.

      4. Borders and shading

      The faint grey gridlines you see on screen don't print. Borders are real lines around your cells: select the table, then All Borders. Fill Colour (the paint bucket) shades cells; shade the heading row so it stands out. One light colour is plenty, and bold headings finish it off.

        Check your understanding: Why pick a light fill colour for the heading row?

        Formatting is for the reader. Dark text on a dark fill is hard to read, which defeats the point of shading it.

        5. Format your leaderboard (v2)

        Here's your leaderboard from last lesson, all eight players. Make it match the brief from class:

        1. Type the heading Total score (3 rounds) in E3. (The column stays empty until next lesson.)
        2. Merge & Center the title across A1 to E1.
        3. Wrap and centre the headings in row 3 (A3 to E3).
        4. Put All Borders on A3 to E11.
        5. Shade the heading row and make it bold.

          Your work saves on this computer as you go.

          Finished early?

          Shade every second player row in a light colour, so your eye can follow a row across. In real Excel, try the Format Painter (the paintbrush): it copies formatting from one cell to another.

          6. Save a new version

          Before you change anything in real Excel, open your v1 file and straight away use File > Save As. Change v1 to v2 in the name. Now v1 stays safe as your backup, and all today's changes go into v2. If something goes badly wrong, v1 is still there.

          Check your understanding: You open Smith_Jo_Tournament_v1 to format it. What should you do first?

          Save As first means every change lands in v2 and v1 stays untouched as a backup. That's called version control, and it's a pre-VET file management skill too.

          Glossary

          Format
          Change how a cell looks without changing the data in it.
          Merge & Center
          Joins selected cells into one and centres the contents. Click again to split them.
          Wrap Text
          Lets long text run onto extra lines inside one cell.
          Border
          A real line around a cell. Unlike gridlines, borders print.
          Fill Colour (shading)
          The background colour of a cell. Keep it light.
          Save As
          Saves a copy under a new name, so the old version stays as a backup.
